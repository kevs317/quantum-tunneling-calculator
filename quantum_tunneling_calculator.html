Interactive visualization

Normalized relative wave-function amplitude |ψ(x)/ψ(0)| = exp(−κx) and probability-density ratio |ψ(x)|²/|ψ(0)|² = exp(−2κx), inside the barrier. The horizontal axis is distance in penetration depths, x/(1/κ). For the door example, the full barrier is enormously wider than this plot.

Validation and assumptions (parts a–e)

(a) Assumptions: one-dimensional, nonrelativistic particle; rectangular, static barrier; constant mass; single fixed particle energy; V > E; exponential approximation; no reflected-wave or boundary-matching calculation; no environmental interactions. The graph shows relative decay inside the barrier, not a fully normalized scattering-state probability density.

(b) AI prompt used: “Create an interactive quantum tunneling calculator using κ = sqrt(2m(V−E))/ℏ and T ≈ exp(−2κL). Let users change mass, energy, barrier height and width; support eV/J and nm/m; graph relative wave-function amplitude and probability density; validate the copper electron and student-door examples; show logarithmic probabilities without numerical underflow.”

(c) Validation: Copper: m = 9.1093837×10⁻³¹ kg, V = 4.7 eV, E = 2.7 eV gives penetration depth ≈ 0.138 nm, agreeing with Problem 7(b). The chosen width of 0.3 nm is an illustrative extra input; 7(b) did not specify a barrier width.

(d) Share: This HTML file is standalone; open it in a browser or share the file.

(e) Extension: Student mass = 60 kg, speed = 2.5 m/s, so E = ½mv² = 187.5 J. Door V = 25,000 J and L = 0.03 m. The result is log₁₀(T) ≈ −4.26×10³⁵, or T ≈ exp(−9.82×10³⁵). This cannot be represented as an ordinary floating-point probability. The logarithmic result remains meaningful.
